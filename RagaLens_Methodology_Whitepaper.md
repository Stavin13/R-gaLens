# RagaLens: Computational Methodology Whitepaper
**Authoritative Procedure for Music Academy Journal Analysis (1930-2023)**

---

## 1. Executive Summary
This document outlines the technical and musicological procedures used to develop **RagaLens**, a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) system designed for deep-dive historical research into the *Music Academy Journals*. The methodology ensures that semantic searches across 90 years of archives are accurate, scholarly, and context-aware.

## 2. Data Acquisition & Corpus Details
*   **Source Material**: 83 volumes of the Music Academy Journals, spanning from 1930 to 2023.
*   **Format**: PDF documents containing historical manuscripts, research papers, and conference reports.
*   **Temporal Scope**: 93 years of musicological transition.

## 3. Data Pre-processing Pipeline
To transform historical documents into a searchable AI memory, the following steps were taken:

### 3.1 Text Extraction
Using the **pdfminer.six** library, text was extracted from the multi-column and often aged PDF layouts. Extra care was taken to maintain the reading order across pages.

### 3.2 Recursive Chunking
Text was split into semantic units called "chunks" (approx 1000 characters each). 
*   **Overlap**: A 200-character overlap was maintained between chunks to ensure that a musical concept (like a *Raga Lakshana*) wasn't cut in half, preserving its semantic context for the AI.

### 3.3 Metadata Tagging
Every chunk was tagged with:
*   **Source Filename** (e.g., `Vol.4_1933.pdf`)
*   **Publication Year** (extracted via regex patterns)
*   **Page Number**
This internal tagging allows the AI to provide exact scholarly citations for every claim.

## 4. Semantic Vector Architecture
The core "intelligence" of the search is handled by converting text into numerical vectors.

### 4.1 Embedding Model
We utilized the **`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`** SentenceTransformer. This model was chosen for its high efficiency and its ability to understand the semantic proximity of musicological concepts (e.g., knowing that "Taala" and "Rhythm" are related even if the words differ).

### 4.2 Vector Database (FAISS)
The 384-dimensional vectors were indexed using **FAISS (Facebook AI Similarity Search)**. This allows for nearly instant semantic retrieval across the entire 90-year archive by using Euclidean distance to find the most relevant historical passages.

## 5. The RAG Retrieval Logic
The system uses a custom-built processing chain in **FastAPI**:

### 5.1 Synonym Expansion Layer
To account for 90 years of changing spellings and transliterations from Sanskrit, queries are expanded dynamically:
*   **Logic**: A query for "Desi" automatically searches for: `desi`, `deshi`, `deśi`, `deśya`. 
*   **Impact**: This uncovers historical research that would be missed by a standard keyword search.

### 5.2 Contextual Relevancy (Top-K)
The system retrieves the top 10–15 most relevant historical excerpts for every query, providing a dense "knowledge bundle" to the Large Language Model.

## 6. Scholarly Response Generation
The final answer is generated by **Llama-3.3-70b-versatile** via the Groq Inference Engine.

### 6.1 System Prompting
The AI is strictly constrained with a "Senior Musicologist" persona. It is instructed to:
1.  Prioritize evidence found in the journals.
2.  Contrast findings across different years (e.g., "In 1942, the focus was X, but by 2009 it shifted to Y").
3.  Always provide exact citations (Volume & Page).

### 6.2 Hardware Stability (Mac ARM)
The backend is optimized for **Apple Silicon (arm64)** by limiting multithreading (`OMP_NUM_THREADS=1`), preventing the race conditions that typically crash heavy AI libraries on Mac machines.

## 7. Conclusion
This computational methodology moves musicological research beyond simple "Ctrl+F" keyword searching. By utilizing semantic vector space and historical year-tagging, researchers can now perform automated cross-temporal studies on the evolution of Indian music traditions.
